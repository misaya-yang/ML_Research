## One unknown-scale identity

Let S=Z dP/dQ, with unknown Z and fixed raw-score cutoff B chosen only on the independent proposal nomination stream. Mark a target sample active with probability g(S)=min(1,B/S); accept a proposal sample with probability a(S)=min(1,S/B). Since g dP=(B/Z)a dQ, conditional active/accepted h shares Bernoulli center c, and m=P h=(1-r)c+r c_R for r=P(inactive) and c_R=P(h=1|inactive). The same proof holds for sharp g(S)=1{S≤B}, a(S)=(S/B)1{S≤B}. Clopper–Pearson bounds for r, c_R and c apply to conditional accepted counts. They do not need Z. Reusing the P-only nomination states for marginal center confidence is okay because the cutoff is independent of that P stream; the method does not need center coverage conditional on the chosen gate order, only unconditional coverage intersected with conditionally valid path tests.

For size nP, conditional on a Q estimate c_hat, the alternative residual estimator c_hat+nP^-1Σ R_i(h_i−c_hat) has conditional P variance at most r/nP and conditional bias (1−r)(c_hat−c). Conditional on N≥1 accepted Q draws, c_hat is a Bernoulli average of N; N~Bin(nQ,Q a). Thus the usual Bernstein bound gives target-label term sqrt(2r log(1/δ)/nP)+O(log(1/δ)/nP), plus overlap-c error. This is a center contribution, not sample complexity of certifying the entire path risk.

## Results

The split version uses independent Q calibration and path banks; endpoint tests suffice because conditional on all calibration and the true landscape the unsafe endpoint is fixed. At equal additional Q score caps 8192 / 20480 / 69632, P-only CP certified 13 / 20 / 23, bridge 5 / 20 / 23, sharp 7 / 19 / 22, and exact-center oracle 17 / 20 / 27. No selected risk point exceeds .5. Hybrid used 4096 of its Q budget on calibration per task, which substantially reduced low-budget path audit power.

The shared-score version eliminates it. At a fixed cap, use the same Q rows for overlap and true-path e-tests. Verify a conservative minimum of the path e-process over the entire random center set, not merely at random endpoints. True m is fixed and in the set on the coverage event, so this cannot be larger than the test evidence at m. Each betting factor's lower bound over a center subinterval uses the smaller endpoint value of the (concave) negative quadratic gap, and upper/lower bounds for the positive denominator according to the numerator sign. This handles arbitrarily dependent center and path data; valid conditional e-values are evaluated at a fixed prefix. Gates use original independent nomination and stop at the first failure. ## Why the hard cases fail

At Q=20480 every method including exact-center oracle rejects the same twelve tasks at the first (b,t)=(32,128) gate. Exact m is .9440–.98223. On these cases, |2(1+.5)(m−m*)| for m*=(E[time average]+.25)/1.5 is ≥.4467 (median .4753 using Rao–Blackwell means); none exhibits near-quadratic cancellation. At Q=69632 five exact-m tasks still fail, all m≥.97779 and the same first gate. Independent fresh 1024 actual Q-start trajectories per hard cell confirm accepted post-burn rare-state presence .551–.980 and mean rare occupancy .0179–.0573. Raw Q rare probability .0423–.1651; 160 Q draws contain at least one such proposal .998–1.000. Thus a conditional trick discarding no-raw-rare blocks cannot improve this panel. The universal path-loss range permits all-rare occupancy and forces the bounded-risk betting test to pay for long window trajectories, even though most accepted rare visits are short. Genuine fixed caps are score counts, not computational latency. Longer caps and cheaper Q process sampling address a distinct limitation from borrowing target-mean information.

## Tail truncation: promising only with the exact tail oracle

For a Q-start path of B=b+t states with rare observable h=1, choose fixed raw-score cutoff C. On no raw proposal with h=1,S>C (including initial state), couple rare occupancy to a two-state process that enters on every rare proposal (probability a=Q[h=1]), and exits on h=0 if U≤min(1,S/C), probability β=E_Q[1(h=0)min(1,S/C)]. This dominates the real indicator pointwise under those proposals. Use Q rare a as initial probability and transition matrix [[1-a,a],[β,1-β]]. Its count distribution after burn is a finite recursion. For θ≥m², apply the monotone envelope to [(X−m)²−θ]_+, add ((1−m)²−θ)*P(any high-rare proposal) (with positive part in multiplier), and use Jensen if clipping the Rao–Blackwell conditional mean of the squared error. This compares directly to the original unmodified chain MSE. In the finite run the dominating a is clipped upward CP, β clipped downward CP, and tail upper CP enters as 1−(1−qt_upper)^B. Independence is only required for the full actual candidate block; the envelopes use a union allocation and separately disclosed pilot.

Optimistic 262,144-Q plug-in screen suggests six of the twelve hard cases can cut range to 8.1% or less if the tail allowance is 0.1 target variance. But the finite validation screen used a separate 8,192-Q cutoff pilot, nine predeclared cutoffs, and union CP bounds at α=.015 for both per-cut quantities and common rare rate. Even gifting exact m, at available validation caps 69,632 and 131,072, 0/12 cases halve the range at <=0.10 times target variance tail allowance. At 245,760 Q, 4/12 halve the range. Allowing .25 tail at 69,632 only gives 2/12 half-range and consumes half of the certificate threshold. The Q-tail upper-bound cost, amplified over 160 raw states, is binding despite many bulk rare states. No full-pipeline clipping win claimed. Scripts truncation_diagnose.py and truncation_confidence.py; details in the two truncate CSVs.
