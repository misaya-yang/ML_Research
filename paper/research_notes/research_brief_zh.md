# Cold-Start IMH Auditing: Current Research Brief

**Question:** Given an independent Metropolis–Hastings chain initialized from its proposal, when does the ordinary time average have expected MSE below half the target variance? This is a certificate for a fixed chain, not a guarantee about one realized trajectory or an estimate of a fixed mean.

## Two complementary theoretical results

1. **Instance-specific power with a fixed target quota.** Pro gives optimal certification power in closed form. Its limit is set by the minimum target mass that must be hidden to move this particular safe instance past the true threshold. We checked conditioning in both directions and added an **equality counterexample**: for a four-step fair binary chain, the original power cap can persist despite arbitrarily many proposal scores even when the normalizer upper bound equals the critical value. This does not classify all equality cases. [Theorem and proof](../../theory/proofs/fixed_target_power.md)
2. **Worst-case two-source frontier at finite budgets.** Target centering, cold-chain dynamics, and normalization impose distinct necessary constraints. In the proved long-window regime, the unknown-scale constraint only tightens when the weight floor is much smaller than the overlap floor. Rejection-sampling attempt counts can also estimate scale when available, but their generation must be paid for. Pro's earlier higher-moment result applies in a different regime and is not combined into an unconditional theorem. [Two-source frontier](../../theory/proofs/long_horizon_two_source_frontier.md)

## Current empirical assessment

- Of 16 preregistered near-threshold tasks, 8 are safe and 8 unsafe. Accepted samples alone certified 6/8 safe cases; observing rejections certified 7/8; a reliable scale interval certified 8/8. No false certifications occurred. A strong black-box comparator also certified 8/8 with more scores. **With disclosed HMM factors, dynamic programming resolves all 16 without sampling.** [Per-case results](../../results/disclosure/README.md)
- Pro's structured GUM control requires no independent target samples across 49 cases. TinyStories' only validated certification was matched by a zero-target-sample comparator exploiting disclosed normalization. Withholding public information does not establish a method advantage.
- The higher-order-reward CPU control covers 2.09 million possible sequences, but exact normalization did not beat the strong raw-score comparator on six frozen short-window tasks. With a disclosed proposal and pointwise scoring, 64–512 high-probability sequences per task suffice to decide all six without target samples. Affordable proposal-mass coverage matters more than nominal state count. Screen for more diffuse mass before a large experiment. This is the ICML theory track, not evidence for universal deployment speedups or Oral-level performance.

## What proposal scores recover with a fixed target sample budget

- Pro's construction with known Z and matched weight marginals: two chains share Q, normalization, and the full weight distributions under P and Q. Moving only the observable labels on rare high-weight states gives one safe and one unsafe chain at the same budget. Near a threshold crossing, a bounded moment of order 1+r, a fixed window, and an interior root give sufficient budget O(M^(1/r) p^(1+1/r)+L p²) to reach a fixed fraction of ideal power. The p exponent has a matching lower bound at a fixed window. Neither a uniform bound across all windows nor a per-chain cost statement follows.
- Claude's centering frontier requires moderate overlap and regular tail profiles satisfying integral or doubling control. A point value Ψ(q/p) is insufficient for arbitrary tails: Pro's sufficient condition uses a truncated integral. An independently calibrated four-step half-variance counterexample shows that several weight levels contribute together at the critical 1/W tail, so the pointwise tail misses a logarithmic factor. The pointwise 1/ε target-sample result requires quantified conditions on a simple root and a sufficiently large proposal-scoring budget. Finite q does not imply a uniform lower bound.
- The preregistered open-vocabulary GSM8K temperature crossover was 0/8. Adding target samples certified 114/127 strictly safe windows; retaining the proposal logs certified 114/127 at equal collection cost, although not the same individual windows. Three zero-MH-transition windows met the mean-MSE threshold but their correctness probabilities fell at least 73% below target. Terminal-readout bias belongs in its own deployment requirement; the MSE ratio for a mean is not generation quality or a mixing certificate. [Controls and scope](../../results/disclosure/README.md)

This brief summarizes Pro's current research and reproduction materials. The English PDF is an older proposal-start draft, does not include all these results, and has not been submitted. The next test concerns which real external P interface provides information that complete Q logs cannot recover at equal cost, not expanding GPU experiments for a proxy sample bank.

## Short tails and cold starts: Verified boundaries

- **A long-window dynamics lower bound does not require weights of order T².** At the half-variance threshold the largest weight can be moved to 3T while holding target mean and variance fixed. Exact rational gridding of the four-state continuous limit and a continuity error bound verify that the risk falls from above 0.5035 to below 0.4962 and the derivative stays below −0.0922 across the whole overlap interval. A uniform O(1/T) error transfers this to finite chains. This gives the dynamics witness, **not** matching centering, dynamics, and scale bounds for the same short-tail class. [Dynamics derivation and verification](../../theory/proofs/long_horizon_two_source_frontier.md)
- **Cold starts can give large relative error without heavy tails.** In a two-state example, the proposal is uniform, the target places probability T⁻³ on the low-weight state and the balance on its complement, and the observable detects only the low-weight state. All weights are below 2; without burn-in the exact MSE at T=32 is 96.0742 times the target variance. Conversely, a rare high-weight state can invalidate an unweighted tail bound even at stationarity. The proved form is a stationary tail weighted by the squared task observable plus a cold-start correction, not ordinary tail mass times target variance.
- An independent two-source truncated bridge locates a centering cutoff within a constant factor without simultaneous confidence intervals over a grid. This requires at least half the target mass below q/p, appropriate tails, and the stated budgets. It eliminates the log log cost for cutoff location, not the entire short-to-long window transition or the need for empirical evidence.

### Cold-start risk: Aligning upper and lower bounds

Claude's Γ bound controls **absolute risk**. Pro's observable-weighted bound separates long high-weight visits from low-weight initial states, enabling relative-risk analysis. Neither replaces the other. Identifying the pre-regeneration prefix as a truncated IMH gives an exact risk decomposition at the first marked step. The stationary term can be integrated using projection energy at each weight threshold. Both identities match direct path-moment recursion in 96 exact rational CPU checks. At 256 steps on the same four-state chain, two observables with identical marginals have relative MSEs 0.11654 and 0.01523, on opposite sides of 1/12. In the labeled, non-addressable two-point model, 95% power with zero false certifications needs 11 P-only rows or 23 Q-only rows. A separate long-window construction even keeps the squared-observable tail fixed: changing only the signed pairing crosses the half-variance threshold. Pro's single-threshold upper bound abstains on this safe instance. **The jointly optimal query frontier for short tails and real-model savings at equal cost remain unproved.** [Cold-start risk proof](../../theory/proofs/cold_risk_bound.md)

### Main result: Two resource boundaries at fixed small overlap

The result proves the whole budget region for a common public assumption class. Let N=ε⁻² log(1/δ)/v. With target mean .5, disclosed variance and overlap lower bound, normalized scores, independent samples, and high precision, the two necessary and sufficient conditions up to constants are `nP+nQ/T ≳ N` and `nQ ≳ N/T²`. Proposal-only auditing takes about TN rows. The mixed certificate takes about N target rows and N/T² irreplaceable proposal rows. When rows have equal prices, the savings factor is of order T. At lower precision, roughly above 1/(T√v) with fixed overlap, the second condition may disappear. Prices refer to independently drawn scored rows, not model wall time. Target rows produced by local rejection sampling are not a free independent source. [Fixed-overlap resource frontier](../../theory/proofs/cold_risk_bound.md#fixed-overlap-resource-frontier-for-cold-imh)

### First passage: From a fixed mean to a joint budget

A different question is to preserve the original inference policy's first passing time, output, and verifier ledger. Geometric refreshes and a reverse first-passage decomposition support exact jumps. At matched capacity, the corrected-label 14× fixed-archive test separates joint certification from the product of matched independent marginals and saves 40.51% of residual transition queries after charging single-label counterfactual continuations. The original 9× result failed after correction and is not positive evidence.
